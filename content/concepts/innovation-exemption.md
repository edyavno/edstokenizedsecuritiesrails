*In plain English: The SEC now lets a few approved onchain venues trade tokens of US stocks, for a limited time and under conditions. This page explains how that works and who objects.*

On 17 Sep 2026 the SEC issued an order (Rel. 34-106402, File 4-927) granting temporary, conditional section 36(a)(1) relief ([SEC order](https://business.cch.com/srd/34-106402091726.pdf); [press release](https://business.cch.com/srd/SECgov_SECIssuesInnovationExemptiontoFacilitatetheTradingofTokenizedNMSStockandRequestforComment091726.pdf)):
- **Tokenized Securities Venues (TSVs)** operating permissioned [[concepts/glossary#amm-automated-market-maker|AMM]] liquidity pools are exempt from the definition of "exchange".
- **Covered Firm** liquidity providers are exempt from the definition of "dealer".
- **Conditions** include U.S. person and OFAC compliance, a 30-day public notice, issuer notice and objection for third-party tokens, same rights as the underlying, public auditable [[concepts/glossary#smart-contract|smart contracts]] on a permissionless ledger, and synchronized halts ([SEC order](https://business.cch.com/srd/34-106402091726.pdf)).
- **Caps**: Tier 1 cannot exceed 75 symbols and 0.25 percent of prior-month average daily share volume; Tier 2 cannot exceed 250 symbols and 2.5 percent ([SEC order](https://business.cch.com/srd/34-106402091726.pdf); [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/sec-innovation-exemption-establishes)).
- Synthetics are excluded; the order expires five years after publication ([SEC order](https://business.cch.com/srd/34-106402091726.pdf)).

Chairman Atkins framed it as a bridge to durable rulemaking ([statement](https://business.cch.com/srd/SECgov_StatementontheInnovationExemption_ABridgeTowardDurableRulemaking091726.pdf)). Law-firm analyses: [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/sec-innovation-exemption-establishes), [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/sec-s-innovation-exemption-opens-path-tokenized-stock-trading), [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20261005-the-innovation-exemption-arrives-sec-clears-a-conditional-path-for-onchain-trading-of-tokenized-nms-stocks).

## TSV notices
The SEC lists four TSV notices as of 9 Nov 2026 (page last reviewed or updated that day): TSV LLC (23 Sep 2026), OKXICE TSV (4 Oct 2026), Kephas Corporation d/b/a Perpetuals.com (14 Oct 2026) and Formation Securities LLC (9 Nov 2026) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets/innovation-exemption)). Inclusion on the list does not mean the person meets the TSV definition or the exemption conditions ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets/innovation-exemption)). TSV LLC revised its notice on 19 Oct (v1.1) and 23 Oct 2026 (v1.2): it will run on Canton with USDC and SBC as paired assets at commencement, and the issuer's transfer agent handles participant verification and minting ([TSV LLC](https://www.tokenizedstockvenue.com/notice.html)). Version 1.3 (29 Oct 2026) names its first stock, tokenized Canton Strategic Holdings (Nasdaq: CNTN), with Fairmint TA LLC as transfer agent; because the issuer's own transfer agent tokenizes the shares with the issuer's authorization, the third-party objection window does not apply ([TSV LLC notice v1.3](https://www.tokenizedstockvenue.com/notice.html)). CNTN's 8-K press release said trading was expected as early as 2 Nov 2026 ([CNTN 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861657/000149315226049196/ex99-1.htm)); first trades of issuer-sponsored tokenized CNTN on TSV LLC were announced on 4 Nov 2026 (see [[platforms-venues/tsv-llc|TSV LLC]]). [[platforms-venues/formation-securities|Formation Securities]] published a 9 Nov 2026 notice for a Solana-based permissioned Orca AMM; trading had not commenced as of that notice ([Formation notice](https://www.formationsecurities.com/notice.pdf)). On 11 Nov 2026 OKX announced that [[platforms-venues/okxice-tsv|OKXICE TSV]] is due to begin trading on 23 Nov 2026 for eligible U.S. customers, with more than 70 assets expected to be listed; the post does not say every asset will be available on day one ([OKX](https://www.okx.com/en-us/learn/okxice-tsv-launch)).

## Issuer objection
For third-party tokens, the issuer gets notice and can object during the 30-day window ([SEC order](https://business.cch.com/srd/34-106402091726.pdf)). Explainer applied to the first notice: [Dida Labs](https://didalabs.com/tokenized-stock-objection/) (analysis). First test: by 21 Oct 2026, 16 issuers, including Goldman Sachs, Microsoft, Alphabet and McDonald's, had objected to OKXICE listings ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/tokenized-stocks-us-companies-object.html), news). By OKXICE's 28 Oct notice the count was 38, including JPMorgan, Coca-Cola, Walmart and Eli Lilly ([OKXICE notice, 28 Oct](https://www.okx.com/cdn/assets/okfe/web3-activities/okxicetsv/notices/OKXICE_Tokenized_Securities_Venue_Public_Notice_26.10.28.pdf)); by its 30 Oct notice it was 50, adding NVIDIA, Costco, Micron and NIKE among others ([OKXICE notice, 30 Oct](https://www.okx.com/cdn/assets/okfe/web3-activities/okxicetsv/notices/OKXICE_Tokenized_Securities_Venue_Public_Notice_26.10.30.pdf)). As of the 5 Nov 2026 notice, the list had 67 issuers (Alphabet counted once) ([OKXICE notice, 5 Nov](https://www.okx.com/cdn/assets/okfe/web3-activities/okxicetsv/notices/OKXICE_Tokenized_Securities_Venue_Public_Notice_26.11.05.pdf)). This page uses that notice's count of 67.

How the opt-out works for issuers, per Nasdaq's FAQ ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/docs/listings/faq-sec-innovation-exemption), last updated 29 Sep 2026): the TSV must send notice to the address of the issuer's principal executive offices on its Exchange Act reports; the issuer has 30 calendar days from receipt to object; the objection goes directly to that venue, and Nasdaq can't file it for them. Notices and [[concepts/glossary#opt-out-issuer-objection|opt-outs]] are per TSV, with no blanket opt-out. Nasdaq says it intends to opt Nasdaq, Inc. out of TSVs, and warns that TSVs don't have to show best prices or report trades until 10 minutes after they happen.

The contrast CNBC draws: offshore volume is mostly perps and [[concepts/glossary#wrapper|wrapped]] tokens that never needed issuer permission, while the US path gives issuers a say ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/tokenized-stocks-us-companies-object.html)). See [[platforms-venues/okxice-tsv]].

## What can a company's objection do and not do?

A timely objection can keep that company's stock off *that* Tokenized Securities Venue, when the token would be a third-party token. It does not, by itself, block every other way the same stock might be tokenized or traded.

- **30-day window, per venue.** For third-party tokens, the TSV must send notice to the issuer, and the issuer has 30 calendar days from receipt to object. The objection goes to that venue. Notices and opt-outs are per TSV; there is no blanket opt-out ([SEC order 34-106402](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-106402.pdf); [Nasdaq issuer FAQ](https://www.nasdaq.com/docs/listings/faq-sec-innovation-exemption), last updated 29 Sep 2026).
- **Not a bar on issuer-sponsored or DTC routes.** Those are separate paths. An issuer-sponsored token is tokenized with the issuer's authorization (for example, CNTN on TSV LLC, where the third-party objection window does not apply because the issuer's own transfer agent tokenizes the shares). DTC Tokenized Entitlements are minted on a participant's instruction against securities already held at DTC. An objection at one TSV does not, by itself, change those routes.
- **OKXICE's objector count.** As of OKXICE's 5 Nov 2026 notice, 67 issuers had objected (Alphabet counted once). This page uses that notice's count of 67, consistent with the [[platforms-venues/okxice-tsv|OKXICE]] page ([OKXICE notice, 5 Nov](https://www.okx.com/cdn/assets/okfe/web3-activities/okxicetsv/notices/OKXICE_Tokenized_Securities_Venue_Public_Notice_26.11.05.pdf)).
- **What an objection does not automatically stop.** Offshore wrapped tokens and equity perpetual futures have not, in the sources cited on this page, required this TSV objection. CNBC's contrast, already noted above, is that offshore volume is mostly perps and wrapped tokens that never needed issuer permission, while the US TSV path gives issuers a say ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/tokenized-stocks-us-companies-object.html)).

## What are senators asking the SEC? (13 Nov 2026)
Four Senate Democrats, Angela Alsobrooks, Mark Warner, Ruben Gallego and Catherine Cortez Masto, wrote to Chairman Atkins in a letter dated 13 Nov 2026 and released 16 Nov ([letter](https://www.alsobrooks.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Alsobrooks_Warner_Cortez_Masto_Gallego_SEC_Innovation_Exemption_Tokenization_Letter_11-12-2026_@_02-10_PM.pdf); [press release](https://www.alsobrooks.senate.gov/news/press-releases/alsobrooks-leads-colleagues-in-pushing-sec-for-answers-transparency-on-tokenized-stock-change/)). They start from "a tokenized security is a security" and say the order lacks clarity in three areas:
- **Illicit finance.** Beyond requiring a TSV to be a U.S. person subject to OFAC sanctions, the order "sets no identity-verification standard and does not require an AML program." It permissions access to trading "but does not require transfer restrictions in smart contracts at the token level," so a token bought by a verified person could move to an unverified wallet. This is the same gap Securitize's letter raises below.
- **Issuers.** The letter says over sixty issuers have objected (its figure, citing CFO Brew) and that there is no apparent way to lodge a standing objection, object after the 30-day window, or rescind consent, and no consolidated public list of TSV notices.
- **Investors.** It asks who owes best execution when an investor trades directly on a TSV, whether SIPC coverage and advertising rules apply, and notes that TSVs need not follow [[concepts/glossary#reg-nms|Reg NMS]].

The senators ask for 13 answers within thirty days and for any permanent change to go through notice-and-comment rulemaking. A letter from legislators is not law and does not change the order.

## When will permanent rules come?
SEC Trading and Markets Director Jamie Selway, who said his remarks were his own views and not the Commission's, told Unchained's livestream on 16 Nov 2026 that the staff is "framing it up now" and "that's got to be a 2027 event in terms of getting a proposal out there" ([video at 1:10:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwAidpXpJJ4&t=4255s); [Unchained write-up](https://unchainedcrypto.com/secs-jamie-selway-says-a-permanent-tokenized-stock-rule-proposal-has-got-to-be-a-2027-event/)). He said the five-year term was chosen to "keep us on the hook" to do permanent rulemaking ([37:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwAidpXpJJ4&t=2259s)), and that he "certainly wouldn't foreclose" those rules extending to tokenized derivatives ([40:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwAidpXpJJ4&t=2450s)). On synthetic stock tokens sold offshore, he said US investors accessing them is "not compliant" and that action could be taken ([1:04:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwAidpXpJJ4&t=3851s)). Quotes are from the auto-generated captions, checked against the write-up.

## Industry response
A week after the order, Bullish and Equiniti formed the [[concepts/issuer-sponsored-token-coalition|Issuer Sponsored Token Coalition]], citing the requirement that venues verify a token gives holders the same rights as the traditional share ([Bullish](https://www.bullish.com/us/news-insights/leading-market-infrastructure-firms-form-coalition-to-advance-issuer-sponsored-tokenized-securities)).

## Comment file (4-927)
Better Markets, an investor advocacy nonprofit, filed a 13-page letter on 22 Oct 2026. It calls TSVs and the dealer exemption the largest rewrite of secondary equity trading rules since [[concepts/glossary#reg-nms|Reg NMS]], argues such changes need APA notice-and-comment rulemaking (with cost-benefit analysis) rather than an order, and urges the SEC to withdraw or rescind the order. It also asks that third-party tokens require the issuer's affirmative consent instead of an opt-out ([Better Markets](https://bettermarkets.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Better-Markets-Comment-Letter-SEC-Innovation-Exemption.pdf)).

Before the order, at a 27 Jan 2026 SEC Crypto Task Force meeting with Citadel and JPMorgan, SIFMA warned that broad exemptions for tokenized trading could weaken investor protection and said an innovation exemption is no substitute for notice-and-comment rulemaking ([SEC memo](https://www.sec.gov/files/ctf-memo-sifma-cahill-gordon-reindel-llp-citadel-llc-jpmorgan-chase-co-012726.pdf)).

SIFMA, the broker-dealer and asset manager trade group, filed an initial letter on 19 Oct 2026 (signed by CEO Kenneth Bentsen). Unlike Better Markets it does not ask for withdrawal; it asks the SEC to amend the order and answer operational questions. Its asks: trading of third-party tokens only with the issuer's affirmative consent (opt-in); failing that, an official SEC- or FINRA-run portal listing TSVs, their notices and issuer elections, a 90-day objection period from confirmed receipt, and a single universal issuer opt-out; and the right to object at any time, even after trading starts. It also asks about surveillance (CAT, ISG), volume limits across venues, beneficial ownership and corporate actions, and says it would support the SEC giving venues more time before they begin operating ([SIFMA](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/SIFMA-Innovation-Exemption-Initial-Letter-10-19-2026.pdf)).

Solana Policy Institute filed the first clearly supportive letter on 22 Oct 2026 (signed by CEO Miller Whitehouse-Levine and General Counsel Patrick Wilson). It commends the order and makes one ask: define "public, permissionless distributed ledger" with a functional, control-based test, drawing on the Senate draft of the Clarity Act. Its five factors: open-source code; anyone can read and submit transactions without individual authorization; pre-set, transparent protocol rules; no person or group with persistent or exclusive power to order or validate transactions, decide inclusion, control finality or change the rules; and no unilateral power to censor or grant preferential treatment. It also asks that disclosed, temporary incident responses (such as a network pause or emergency patch) not disqualify a ledger. SIFMA asked for a similar definition ([Solana Policy Institute](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1106380-3893468.pdf)).

Katya Malinova (McMaster) and Andreas Park (Toronto), market-structure academics, filed on 23 Oct 2026 against the volume caps. Their argument: arbitrage trades that keep pool prices in line with the main market count toward the cap, so the cap limits the very thing that fixes price gaps; and capping volume caps the fee income that pays liquidity providers, so costs rise. In their model the lowest attainable trading cost scales with 1/√s for a cap at share s of market volume: about 20 times at the 0.25 percent cap and about 6 times at 2.5 percent. They ask the SEC to remove the caps, or set them per stock, and to study AMM effects on market quality ([Malinova and Park](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1107499-3908312.pdf)).

DeFi Education Fund (16 Oct 2026) also welcomed the order, but notes it covers only intermediated, permissioned venues and asks for rulemaking with safe harbors for developers of decentralized exchanges and their user interfaces ([DeFi Education Fund](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1096319-3852226.pdf)).

Securitize Corp. filed a 7-page letter on 12 Nov 2026 (signed by CEO Carlos Domingo). It supports the order and says that support is not because Securitize needs the relief — it already trades tokenized securities onchain under existing rules — but because it wants AMM-based TSVs tested in the United States. Its main ask is that identity and sanctions screening be enforced in the token itself, not only by a pool allowlist at the point of trade: a verified buyer can withdraw to a self-hosted wallet and send the token to a sanctioned person, and a pool allowlist does not reach that onward transfer. If token-level permissioning is not required, it says the issuer or transfer agent should keep freeze, burn and reissue powers, with a documented legal basis, notice to holders, and audit. It also asks the SEC to explicitly permit PropAMMs (AMMs that reprice from an offchain model, as described by Jump Crypto), to define "permissionless" as a ledger that anyone can join, read, transact on, run a verifying node on, and deploy contracts to without a gatekeeper, and to let issuers affirmatively consent and waive the 30-day wait. The SEC posted the PDF under an S7-2026-32 path; the letter itself is filed in 4-927 ([Securitize](https://www.sec.gov/comments/S7-2026-32/4927-1134705-4042498.pdf)).

Chainlink Labs filed a 10-page letter on 11 Nov 2026 (signed by General Counsel Benjamin Sherwin). Chainlink is a vendor of oracle, market-data and corporate-action services of the kind the letter discusses. It proposes FAQs and nonbinding principles on four topics: that TSVs may use third-party providers to meet the order's conditions; that transaction reports should be comparable across venues, with stable identifiers linking each token to its underlying security, stated USD conversion methods, corrections linked to the original print, and a free open API, flagging the ten-minute window as a comparability problem when several TSVs list the same stock; that TSVs should reconcile corporate-action event data across systems (record dates, differences in finality, transfers in progress at the cutoff) and name who maintains the authoritative entitlement record; and that external data used for pricing and controls should be assessed for suitability, dependencies and what happens on failure ([Chainlink Labs](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1133160-4036827.pdf)).

Five securities-law scholars, including former SEC General Counsel John C. Coates, together with John C. Coffee, Jr., James D. Cox, Merritt B. Fox and Joel Seligman, filed Shadow SEC Statement No. 17 on 10 Nov 2026 (the statement itself is dated 19 Oct 2026). They argue that the order is a legislative rule in substance and should have gone through APA notice-and-comment rulemaking, and that because it does not require connectivity between TSVs or aggregation of quotes or last-sale information, the regime will fragment liquidity and weaken price discovery ([Shadow SEC Statement No. 17](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1131060-4029687.pdf)).

The American Securities Association, a trade group for regional financial services firms, filed on 6 Nov 2026 on the opposing side. It argues that offshore affiliates can supply liquidity as Covered Firms without registration, that the SEC's reach over that offshore activity is uncertain, that the design opens a channel for foreign-adversary actors to launder money into US equities, and that the order treats cybersecurity as a disclosure item when it should require Regulation SCI-level standards. The letter cites a 29 Oct 2026 Binance theft of about $6.36 billion across ten chains, attributed to North Korea's TraderTraitor; those figures were reported by TRM Labs, and ASA also cites CBS News as saying Chainalysis concurs in the attribution ([ASA](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1122019-3994146.pdf)).

Matter Labs Technologies (Prividium/ZKsync) filed a 3-page letter on 4 Nov 2026 asking the SEC to clarify that a TSV can meet the "auditable, public, permissionless ledger" condition with validity (zero-knowledge) proofs verified on a public chain, while execution data stays confidential ([Matter Labs](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1121099-3989526.pdf)).

Coin Center, a crypto policy nonprofit, filed on 17 Nov 2026. It asks for formal guidance recognizing First Amendment limits on the SEC's authority over publishing software, and for the SEC to state that an independent developer does not become part of a TSV merely because a venue later adopts the developer's published smart contracts ([Coin Center](https://www.sec.gov/comments/4-927/4927-1145899-4073428.pdf)).

## Stablecoin legs
TSV pools pair against payment [[concepts/glossary#stablecoin|stablecoins]]; the GENIUS Act (S.1582, P.L. 119-27) defines permitted payment stablecoins and 1:1 reserves ([GENIUS Act](https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582enr.htm)). The Act takes effect on 18 Jan 2027; the OCC's final rule for issuers under its jurisdiction takes effect the same day ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-50.html); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21706/implementing-the-guiding-and-establishing-national-innovation-for-us-stablecoins-act-for-the), FR Doc. 2026-21706).

First notice: [[platforms-venues/okxice-tsv|OKXICE TSV]]. Primitive: [[infrastructure/uniswap-permissioned-pools|Uniswap permissioned pools]]. Uniswap Labs published a 10 Nov 2026 post on how it reads TSV conditions (fee disclosure, access controls, symbol and volume caps, and trading halts) as mappable to Uniswap v4 hooks and the Permissioned Pools hook; the post says Uniswap Labs does not operate any TSV ([Uniswap](https://blog.uniswap.org/building-a-tokenized-securities-venue-on-uniswap)).
