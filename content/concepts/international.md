*In plain English: How rules for tokenized securities differ outside the US, region by region.*

**EU [[concepts/glossary#dlt|DLT]] Pilot Regime**: ESMA's 2025 review found low uptake, with only [[concepts/glossary#csd|CSD]] Prague, 21X and 360X authorised; barriers include thresholds, interoperability, central-bank money and legal uncertainty. ESMA recommends making the regime permanent and wider ([ESMA report](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/ESMA75-117376770-460_Report_on_the_functioning_and_review_of_the_DLTR_-_Art.14.pdf); [ESMA press](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-suggests-amendments-dlt-pilot-regime-make-it-permanent)).
**UK tokenised collateral at CCPs**: on 29 Oct 2026 the Bank of England consulted on draft supervisory expectations for clearing houses (CCPs) that want to accept tokenised versions of collateral they can already take. It says current rules are no barrier and creates no new regime; the token must still be valued, moved and sold when needed, including in stress. Responses close 12 Feb 2027 ([Bank of England CP](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/cp/supervisory-expectations-for-ccps-seeking-to-accept-tokenised-collateral)).
**EU central bank money settlement (Pontes)**: on 26 Oct 2026 the European Stability Mechanism (ESM) settled a €1bn digital benchmark bond through Pontes, the Eurosystem platform that settles DLT trades in central bank money (live since 21 Sep 2026). Clearstream ran the bond on its D7 DLT platform with the cash leg paid in TARGET (T2); the Bundesbank was issuing and paying agent. The bond can move to traditional settlement via T2S and is expected to be eligible as Eurosystem and Eurex collateral ([Deutsche Börse release](https://www.deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/media/news-stories/press-releases/ESM-is-first-European-supranational-issuer-to-settle-DLT-based-bond-issuance-on-Eurosystem-s-Pontes-platform-with-Bundesbank-and-Clearstream-5660764)).
**South Korea**: revised securities laws recognise security tokens as securities from 4 Feb 2027. The FSC's Sep 2026 roadmap starts with institutional money market funds and bonds, unlisted stocks via trusts and public fractional securities, then all public securities, then stablecoin-linked on-chain payments ([FSC roadmap](https://fsc.go.kr/eng/pr010101/87653)). Draft rules (comments to 11 Nov 2026) require ledgers shared by two or more account managers plus the central depository KSD, let issuers run customer accounts with KRW4bn of equity, and cap retail buying at KRW100m net a year per OTC exchange ([FSC draft rules](https://fsc.go.kr/eng/pr010101/87834)).
**Australia**: the Reserve Bank of Australia consulted (3 Sep to 30 Oct 2026) on how its settlement system RITS could support tokenised markets: linking token platforms to RITS for [[concepts/glossary#dvp|DvP]], at-par exchange of tokenised money, reserve access for [[concepts/glossary#stablecoin|stablecoin]] issuers, and tokenised reserves. It sets out asset-lock, reserves-lock and combined-lock models without choosing one. This is a payments paper, not a securities rule ([RBA consultation paper](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/tokenised-money/the-role-of-rits-in-supporting-settlement-in-a-tokenised-ecosystem/pdf/the-role-of-rits-in-supporting-settlement-in-a-tokenised-ecosystem.pdf)).
**Offshore issuer domiciles** in this wiki: Jersey (xStocks, Robinhood), BVI (Ondo), ADGM (bStocks, Coinbase). See [[topics/regulation|regulation]].
Not yet collected: MiCA/[[concepts/glossary#mifid-ii|MiFID II]] detail, UK [[concepts/glossary#digital-securities-sandbox|Digital Securities Sandbox]], Swiss DLT Act, Singapore, Hong Kong.
