*In plain English: How rules for tokenized securities differ outside the US, region by region.*

**EU [[concepts/glossary#dlt|DLT]] Pilot Regime**: ESMA's 2025 review found low uptake, with only [[concepts/glossary#csd|CSD]] Prague, 21X and 360X authorised; barriers include thresholds, interoperability, central-bank money and legal uncertainty. ESMA recommends making the regime permanent and wider ([ESMA report](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/ESMA75-117376770-460_Report_on_the_functioning_and_review_of_the_DLTR_-_Art.14.pdf); [ESMA press](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-suggests-amendments-dlt-pilot-regime-make-it-permanent)).
**UK tokenised collateral at CCPs**: on 29 Oct 2026 the Bank of England consulted on draft supervisory expectations for clearing houses (CCPs) that want to accept tokenised versions of collateral they can already take. It says current rules are no barrier and creates no new regime; the token must still be valued, moved and sold when needed, including in stress. Responses close 12 Feb 2027 ([Bank of England CP](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/cp/supervisory-expectations-for-ccps-seeking-to-accept-tokenised-collateral)).
**UK enabling amendment (Woolard letter, 18 Nov 2026)**: Chris Woolard, the Wholesale Digital Markets Champion, wrote to the Economic Secretary to the Treasury welcoming an amendment the government had laid to the Financial Services and Markets Bill, "which will enable legislation to adapt to technological change." He highlighted "the need for greater clarity for settlement finality" and "the treatment of registers in the Companies Act, which could prevent the issuance of digitally native equities." His second report is due in July 2027. The letter says the government has laid the amendment; it does not say the amendment has been enacted ([letter](https://www.theglobalcity.uk/PositiveWebsite/media/research-downloads/20261118_CW-Letter.pdf)).
**EU central bank money settlement (Pontes)**: on 26 Oct 2026 the European Stability Mechanism (ESM) settled a €1bn digital benchmark bond through Pontes, the Eurosystem platform that settles DLT trades in central bank money (live since 21 Sep 2026). Clearstream ran the bond on its D7 DLT platform with the cash leg paid in TARGET (T2); the Bundesbank was issuing and paying agent. The bond can move to traditional settlement via T2S and is expected to be eligible as Eurosystem and Eurex collateral ([Deutsche Börse release](https://www.deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/media/news-stories/press-releases/ESM-is-first-European-supranational-issuer-to-settle-DLT-based-bond-issuance-on-Eurosystem-s-Pontes-platform-with-Bundesbank-and-Clearstream-5660764)).
**South Korea**: revised securities laws recognise security tokens as securities from 4 Feb 2027. The FSC's Sep 2026 roadmap starts with institutional money market funds and bonds, unlisted stocks via trusts and public fractional securities, then all public securities, then stablecoin-linked on-chain payments ([FSC roadmap](https://fsc.go.kr/eng/pr010101/87653)). Draft rules (comments to 11 Nov 2026) require ledgers shared by two or more account managers plus the central depository KSD, let issuers run customer accounts with KRW4bn of equity, and cap retail buying at KRW100m net a year per OTC exchange ([FSC draft rules](https://fsc.go.kr/eng/pr010101/87834)).
**Australia**: the Reserve Bank of Australia consulted (3 Sep to 30 Oct 2026) on how its settlement system RITS could support tokenised markets: linking token platforms to RITS for [[concepts/glossary#dvp|DvP]], at-par exchange of tokenised money, reserve access for [[concepts/glossary#stablecoin|stablecoin]] issuers, and tokenised reserves. It sets out asset-lock, reserves-lock and combined-lock models without choosing one. This is a payments paper, not a securities rule ([RBA consultation paper](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/tokenised-money/the-role-of-rits-in-supporting-settlement-in-a-tokenised-ecosystem/pdf/the-role-of-rits-in-supporting-settlement-in-a-tokenised-ecosystem.pdf)).
**Singapore / US tokenized-deposit rails (cash leg):** on 16 Nov 2026 DBS and Kinexys by J.P. Morgan launched a commercially ready interoperability link for instant USD transfers between blockchain-based accounts in Singapore and the United States, including on weekends. This is tokenized commercial-bank money, not a tokenized security; see [[topics/settlement-and-matching|Settlement & matching]] ([DBS release](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_and_Kinexys_by_JP_Morgan_launch_industry_first_247_interoperability_solution_for_cross_border_tokenised_deposit_transfers)).
**US ZelleUSD stablecoin (cash leg):** on 16 Nov 2026 Zelle demonstrated a $1.25 million confidential ZelleUSD transfer on Solana devnet at Breakpoint, between demo banks labeled Bank Alpha and Bank Beta. This is a [[concepts/glossary#stablecoin|stablecoin]] cash-leg example, not a tokenized deposit or a tokenized security; see [[topics/settlement-and-matching|Settlement & matching]]. Zelle's 11 June 2026 release said India would be the first country where U.S. consumers can use Zelle to send money overseas, with initial availability expected before the end of 2026, but that release does not say the corridor uses ZelleUSD, and no launch date has been given ([Zelle release](https://www.zelle.com/press-releases/zelle-heads-to-india-unveils-zelleusdsm-stablecoin-other-markets), [@solana](https://x.com/solana/status/2122284580405236216)).
**Global (IOSCO, Nov 2026)**: IOSCO's 2026 monitoring report says regulators are converging on legal certainty of token ownership, alignment of onchain and offchain records, custody, disclosure of investor rights, resilience, and governance of smart contracts, bridges and oracles. On stock tokens it says authorities stress distinguishing tokenized shares that confer shareholder rights from products that only give price exposure, which may fall under derivatives, structured-product or other frameworks ([IOSCO 2026](https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD832.pdf)).
**Singapore / Germany (Project Simbolon, cash leg)**: on 16 Nov 2026 MAS and the Deutsche Bundesbank reported a proof of concept for atomic euro/Singapore-dollar payment-versus-payment across two separate central-bank test ledgers, linked by zero-knowledge proofs rather than a shared ledger or trusted intermediary ([MAS release](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-and-deutsche-bundesbank-advance-cross-border-digital-asset-settlement-connectivity); [insights note](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/development/fintech/project-simbolon/project-simbolon-insights-note.pdf)). See [[topics/settlement-and-matching|Settlement & matching]].
**Hong Kong (Project Ensemble and Swift)**: on 16 Nov 2026 the HKMA said it is continuing work with Swift, Bank of China (Hong Kong), Standard Chartered Hong Kong and HSBC to explore interoperability between the Swift ledger and Project Ensemble's settlement layer for multi-currency tokenised-deposit cross-border payments. This is exploration of the cash leg, not a live service ([HKMA](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/11/20261116-4/)).
**Hong Kong (HSBC RedCoin tokenised fund access):** on 19 Nov 2026 HSBC said eligible customers can use HSBC RedCoin (RCHKD), its licensed Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, in the HSBC HK Mobile Banking App to buy Class SD, a new tokenised unit class of the HSBC Global Money Funds – Hong Kong Dollar, from a minimum of HKD 100 (funded with RCHKD 100). HSBC's Securities Services tokenises the class, and its Digital Assets Custody safekeeps both the stablecoin and Class SD. This is a tokenised money market fund, not a tokenized stock ([HSBC](https://www.about.hsbc.com.hk/news-and-media/hsbc-redcoin-expands-into-wealth-with-tokenised-fund-access)).
**Offshore issuer domiciles** in this wiki: Jersey (xStocks, Robinhood), BVI (Ondo), ADGM (bStocks, Coinbase). See [[topics/regulation|regulation]].
**EU DLT Pilot Regime venues (Oct to Nov 2026)**: 21X AG, which describes itself as the first venue licensed under the Pilot Regime to run both trading and settlement, ran a month-long pilot of its fully onchain order book with LayerZero on a private Zero instance; it reports 10,000 order matches per second (company-reported, [21X](https://21x.eu/21x-ag-and-layerzero-deliver-institutional-grade-onchain-trading-pilot-on-private-permissioned-deployment-of-zero/)); see [[platforms-venues/21x|21X]]. On 20 Oct 2026 Boerse Stuttgart Group obtained a DLT trading and settlement system licence from BaFin for Seturion, which lets connected venues offer tokenized assets without their own DLT licence ([Boerse Stuttgart](https://group.boerse-stuttgart.com/en/seturion-blog/boerse-stuttgart-group-obtains-dlt-tss-license-and-lays-regulatory-basis-for-seturion/)); see [[platforms-venues/seturion|Seturion]].
**Dubai (DFSA)**: on 5 Nov 2026 the DFSA published a white paper, *Scaling Tokenisation: The Road Ahead*, on custody, digital settlement, interoperability and cross-border coordination; it says it is not a consultation, policy statement or guidance ([DFSA](https://www.dfsa.ae/news/dfsa-launches-scaling-tokenisation-road-ahead)).
**EU funds (Amundi)**: on 5 Nov 2026 Amundi announced a tokenised share class of a flagship European equity index fund on public Ethereum, built with CACEIS's tokenisation technology and kept within the fund's existing operating model ([Amundi](https://about.amundi.com/article/amundi-extends-tokenisation-index-investing)).
**EU Tokenisation Standards Group**: on 9 Nov 2026 the ECB, the European Commission and ESMA called for nominations to a new industry-chaired European Tokenisation Standards Group, with the three authorities as observers, to develop common standards for an interconnected tokenised financial ecosystem and a framework to monitor compliance. Applications are due 27 Nov 2026 ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/eu-seeks-nominations-stakeholder-group-tokenisation-standards-finance)).
**Ireland (Central Bank of Ireland, DP12 feedback):** in November 2026 the Central Bank of Ireland published a Feedback Statement to Discussion Paper 12 on DLT and tokenisation, with five priority areas led by central bank money settlement and the collateral framework, placed alongside EU reforms (including proposed DLT Pilot Regime changes and replacing the Settlement Finality Directive with a regulation) and the Eurosystem's Pontes and Appia work ([Central Bank of Ireland](https://www.centralbank.ie/docs/default-source/publications/discussion-papers/discussion-paper-12/feedback-statement-discussion-paper12.pdf?sfvrsn=b6b0741a_16)).
**Bermuda**: on 6 Nov 2026 the Bermuda Monetary Authority published a stakeholder letter on its April 2026 asset-tokenisation consultation. It intends to amend existing laws rather than create a separate tokenisation regime, with a single harmonised definition of "tokenised investment" in the Investment Business Act 2003, cross-referenced by the funds, fund administration, DABA and DAIA acts; both "digital twin" and "native token" models recognised, with model-specific expectations; and three functional roles retained (Primary Tokeniser, Secondary Offeror, Custodian). It will not prescribe a chain or token standard. Guidance is to cover cross-chain and bridging risk. The Authority noted feedback that autonomous protocols and non-custodial software should not be treated as Secondary Offerors solely because they facilitate transactions. Legislation, guidance and a transition roadmap are still to come ([BMA](https://www.bma.bm/viewPDF/documents/2026-11-06-14-04-05-Stakeholder-Letter---Feedback-on-the-Consultation-Paper-on-Asset-Tokenisation.pdf)).
**Cambodia**: on 13 Nov 2026 SBI Holdings said it had signed an MOU with the National Bank of Cambodia for a proof of concept on tokenizing Cambodian government bonds on the Canton Network. Under the MOU, the central bank would register and manage bondholder records as tokens transfer; SBI Bank (Cambodia) and SBI Digital Practice would build and run the test environment. The stated aim is small-unit retail holdings through local banks. This is an MOU for a PoC, not a live service; service details and timing are still to be decided ([SBI Holdings](https://www.sbigroup.co.jp/news/pdf/2026/1113_a.pdf)).
**Japan**: the Financial Services Agency has convened a FY2026 study group on digital and decentralised finance, under an "on-chain finance" forum that targets an interim summary and public-private roadmap in early 2027. Topics include stablecoins and tokenised deposits, security tokens (including tokenised stocks and MMFs), DEX/DeFi and wallet providers, the book-entry transfer system, and faster securities settlement. The 20 Oct 2026 secretariat deck says on-chain trading of book-entry securities (JGBs, corporate bonds, investment trusts, stocks and the like) has not been realised in Japan, while domestic tokenisation has begun to broaden from a focus on real-estate securitisation products. It cites a Nomura, Daiwa and three-megabank on-chain DvP pilot, and a "Tokenised Stocks / Tokenised Law WG" set up in November 2025 through the Digital Asset Co-Creation Consortium, whose secretariat is Progmat, Inc. Official agendas record hearings from Nomura Holdings and Securitize Japan (20 Oct 2026) and from JPYC, SBI VC Trade and the Japanese Bankers Association (2 Nov 2026). This is an agenda, not rules ([FSA deck](https://www.fsa.go.jp/singi/digital_2026/shiryo/261020/jimukyoku.pdf); [study group page](https://www.fsa.go.jp/singi/digital_2026/index.html)).
Not yet collected: MiCA/[[concepts/glossary#mifid-ii|MiFID II]] detail, UK [[concepts/glossary#digital-securities-sandbox|Digital Securities Sandbox]], Swiss DLT Act, Singapore and Hong Kong securities rules (the Singapore and Hong Kong items above are cash-leg projects and a tokenised fund, not those rule texts).
